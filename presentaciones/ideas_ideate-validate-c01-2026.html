C1.1
Target opportunity: The policyholder receives a Coverwise response but it doesn't generate enough clarity or confidence to act without calling.
Legend
Selected — part of the chosen solution
Deferred — valid, not the Cycle 01 focus
1
✦ Selected
LLM-Generated Conversational Response
Instead of returning the literal policy text, Coverwise uses an LLM to generate a plain-language response — specific to the policyholder's plan — with a clear coverage verdict.
How it attacks C1.1
Removes policy jargon as a barrier. The response adapts to the user's vocabulary, not the contract's.
Instead of "Article 5.3.2 of your plan grants partial coverage for class-B outpatient procedures," the user sees: "Yes, your traumatology consultation is covered. You only pay the $12,000 copay."
2
✦ Selected
Plan & Certainty Indicator
Each response includes two additional elements: (a) a certainty level ("High confidence — based on your Digital Plus plan") and (b) a clear signal that the answer applies to their specific contract, not a generic one.
How it attacks C1.1
Directly answers "does this apply to MY plan?" — the core trust gap. The policyholder knows the response is theirs, not someone else's.
A green badge at the top of the response: "✓ Applied to your plan: Digital Plus | Certainty: High"
3
✦ Selected
Practical Translation + Next Step
After the response, the system adds a plain-language summary and a concrete suggested action. Format: answer → what this means → what you can do now.
How it attacks C1.1
The trust gap isn't just understanding the answer — it's knowing what to do with it. The next step closes the journey loop inside the digital channel.
"Yes, it's covered. This means you can go to any provider in our network with no prior authorization. Want me to help you find one near you?"
4
Deferred
Intelligent Escalation with Preserved Context
When certainty is low, Coverwise doesn't fail silently. It offers to connect with an advisor and passes the full context: procedure name, plan, and the tentative answer. The advisor picks up without starting from scratch.
How it attacks C1.1
Transforms "I don't trust this" into "there's a human who can confirm this quickly." Converts a frustrated call into a short, contextual one.
Included in the solution design as a low-certainty fallback — not the primary experiment. Will activate only when the LLM confidence score falls below the defined threshold.
5
Deferred
Dual Response: Simple Summary + Technical Detail on Demand
The primary response is short and plain-language. A secondary CTA "View your policy details" expands to show the exact contract text — for users who need the formal source (e.g., to present at a clinic or for supplemental insurance).
How it attacks C1.1
Serves two profiles: the user who trusts the plain summary, and the user who needs the technical text to validate. Doesn't force the first profile to read what confuses the second.
Valid for a power-user segment. Added as a post-validation iteration if SA-5 reveals a meaningful segment that prefers the technical text.
⚡
Ideas 1, 2, and 3 work as a system, not as independent features. Idea 1 provides language clarity. Idea 2 provides personalization confidence. Idea 3 closes the journey with a concrete action. Removing any one of the three leaves the trust gap partially open. The chosen solution is their combination.
