C1.1
Target opportunity: The policyholder receives a Coverwise response but it doesn't generate enough clarity or confidence to act without calling.
1
Solution Decision
✦ Triad decision — Coverwise Conversational Response

Combination of Ideas 1 + 2 + 3

Outcome target: Reduce the % of users who call the call center after receiving a digital response. Move KR3 (NPS post-inquiry) from 28 to ≥ 38 in the first cycle post-build.
1
Idea 1
LLM Conversational Response
Dimension: Language clarity. Eliminates policy jargon. The response adapts to the user's vocabulary.
2
Idea 2
Plan & Certainty Indicator
Dimension: Personalization confidence. Makes it explicit that the answer applies to their specific plan — not a generic one.
3
Idea 3
Next Step Suggestion
Dimension: Journey closure. After the response, a concrete action keeps the user inside the digital channel.
The three ideas attack complementary dimensions of C1.1 and work as a system. SA-7 (LLM accuracy on coverage) is the highest-risk assumption — but it's validable with a quick technical evaluation before build, without requiring user research. Idea 4 (intelligent escalation) is reserved as a fallback for low-certainty cases and is included in the design but not the primary experiment.
IdeaStatusRationale
Idea 4 — Intelligent Escalation Fallback Complementary to the solution. Activates only when LLM certainty falls below the defined threshold. Not the primary experiment.
Idea 5 — Dual Response (simple + technical) Deferred Valid for advanced users. Add as post-validation iteration if SA-5 reveals a meaningful segment that prefers the technical text.
2
Proposed Conversational Flow
1
User input
Policyholder submits a query in colloquial language
e.g., "Do I get coverage for a knee MRI?"
2
Processing — 2 layers
Coverwise processes the query
I1
Semantic layer: maps colloquial vocabulary to the technical procedure (e.g., "knee MRI" → "femoropatellar joint MRI, articular")
I2
LLM layer: generates the response in plain language, personalized to the policyholder's specific plan and certainty level
3
Response — the three elements
What the policyholder sees
I1
Clear verdict: "Yes, it's covered" / "Not covered" / "Needs review"
I2
Plan & certainty indicator: "Applied to your Digital Plus plan · High certainty"
I3
Practical translation + next step: "This means you pay only the $12,000 copay. Want me to help you schedule?"
✓ Applied to your plan: Digital Plus  ·  High certainty
✓ Yes, it's covered.
Your knee MRI (femoropatellar joint) is covered under your Digital Plus plan. You only need to pay the $12,000 copay — no prior authorization required.
→ Find a provider near you
⚠ This information is informational and does not replace NexHealth's formal resolution.
4
Low-certainty fallback (Idea 4)
If certainty falls below the threshold
Intelligent escalation activates: "I'm not 100% sure for your specific case. Want an advisor to confirm it now? They already have your full query." The advisor picks up with full context — no starting from scratch.
3
Experiment Design
Type A/B test in production
Hypothesis If policyholders receive a conversational response personalized to their plan + a suggested next step, then post-inquiry NPS will improve ≥ 5 points and the % of users who call within 24h of a digital inquiry will drop ≥ 20%.
Variants
Control: current response (policy text + PDF)
Variant: Coverwise Conversational Response (Ideas 1+2+3)
Primary metrics
1. NPS post-inquiry (in-app survey, 2 questions)
2. % of calls to call center within 24h of a digital inquiry
Secondary metrics
· Next step CTA adoption rate
· In-session time after the response
· % of queries escalated (low certainty)
Success criteria
SHIP NPS +5 points AND calls reduced ≥ 20% in the first 14 days → ship to 100%
ITERATE NPS improves but calls don't decrease → redesign the next-step flow before shipping
PIVOT Neither metric improves → revisit SA-5 hypothesis (does the user actually prefer plain language?)
Sample / Duration N to be calculated with Paula Castillo (Data Analyst). Estimate: 3–4 weeks for statistical significance given current Coverwise traffic.
4
Pre-Build Assumptions — Validate Before Investing in Development
⚠ These 3 assumptions must be validated before the build begins
SA-5
Policyholders prefer plain language over policy text as their source of confidence.
Risk if false: if the user prefers the technical text as the "official source," the solution may not generate trust and could worsen perception.
SA-6
Policyholders understand and act on the suggested next step after the response.
Risk if false: if the CTA is ignored, the post-response flow needs to be redesigned before the build.
SA-7
The LLM can generate coverage responses that are sufficiently accurate, without hallucinating conditions of the plan.
Risk if false: LLM coverage errors carry legal implications (Chile/Colombia regulation: the response is informational but cannot induce error).
